PROJECT RISK MANAGEMENT 
1.1 
OBJECTIVES  
The purpose of this document is to illustrate the methodology followed by Engineering I.I. in relation to that part 
of Risk Management that falls within Risk Assessment for the project AVANT. This includes: 
 
Risk Identification 
 
Risk Analysis 
 
Risk Evaluation 
 
Risk Treatment 
1.2 
IMPORTANCE  
Many projects fail due to unforeseen challenges. PRM helps you anticipate these issues and take action to: 
 
Increase the chances of success by minimizing the impact of negative events. 
 
Improve decision-making by providing a clear picture of potential risks and their consequences. 
 
Enhance project efficiency by allocating resources effectively to address risks. 
 
Promote proactive problem-solving and a culture of risk awareness within the project team. 
1.3 
BENEFITS  
Implementing a well-defined PRM plan offers several advantages: 
 
Reduced project delays and cost overruns caused by unexpected events. 
 
Improved project predictability and control by proactively managing potential disruptions. 
 
Enhanced stakeholder confidence by demonstrating a commitment to addressing risks. 
 
Increased team comunication and collaboration as everyone works together to manage risks. 
 
Improved learning and knowledge sharing by capturing lessons learned from identified risks.

2.1 
INTRODUCTION 
The following is a more detailed description of the methodology followed by Engineering I.I. for each of the four 
steps in the risk assessment process: 
2.2 
RISK IDENTIFICATION 
Risk identification is the first step in the risk assessment process. It involves identifying all potential risks that 
could affect the project.  
Therefore, the first phase, Risk Identification, is divided into the following activities: 
- 
Risk identification Techiniques 
- 
Risk categories 
- 
Risk Register 
2.2.1 
RISK IDENTIFICATION TECHNIQUES 
This can be done through a variety of methods, such as: 
 
Brainstorming: A brainstorming session is a group meeting where people are encouraged to come up 
with as many ideas as possible, no matter how outlandish they may seem. 
 
Interviews: Interviews can be conducted with project stakeholders to get their input on potential risks. 
 
Surveys: Surveys can be distributed to project stakeholders to gather data on potential risks. 
The risk of an Asset is determined by: 
 
The threat (eventuality that has the potential to generate a loss of asset security directly if it is an 
information asset or indirectly if it is a secondary asset supporting the information asset); 
 
The vulnerability (weakness of the asset or a security control associated with it that can be exploited by 
the threat itself); 
 
The impact on the business if a threat succeeds in exploiting one or more vulnerabilities, generating a 
loss of asset security.

2.3 
RISK ANALYSIS 
Once the risks have been identified, they need to be analyzed to determine their likelihood of occurrence and 
their impact if they do occur. This is done by assigning a probability and a severity rating to each risk. 
 
Probability of occurrence: The probability of occurrence is a measure of how likely it is that the risk will 
actually happen. It is typically assigned a value between 1 and 5, where 1 is the least severe and 5 is the 
most severe . 
 
Severity rating: The severity rating is a measure of the impact of the risk if it does occur. It is typically 
assigned a value between 1 and 5, where 1 is the least severe and 5 is the most severe. 
2.4 
RISK EVALUATION 
Once the risks have been analyzed, they need to be evaluated to determine which ones are the most important. 
This is done by multiplying the probability of occurrence by the severity rating. The risks with the highest scores 
are the ones that need to be addressed first.

2.5 
RISK TREATMENT 
Once the risks have been evaluated, they need to be treated. This can be done in a variety of ways, such as 
avoiding the risk, transferring the risk, mitigating the risk, or accepting the risk. 
 
Avoidance: Avoidance is the best way to deal with a risk if it is possible to avoid it altogether. This might 
involve changing the project scope or design. 
 
Transfer: Transfer is the next best option if it is not possible to avoid the risk. This might involve 
outsourcing the risky activity to a third party or purchasing insurance. 
 
Mitigation: Mitigation is the process of reducing the likelihood of occurrence or the impact of a risk. 
This might involve implementing training, procedures, or controls. 
 
Acceptance: Acceptance is the last resort if it is not possible to avoid, transfer, or mitigate the risk. This 
means that the project team will simply have to live with the risk. 
 
2.6 
RISK COMUNICATION, MONITORING AND CONTROL  
The risk management process foresees continuous monitoring of project activities and regular interaction with 
the Project Management Team (PM)  (at least monthly through regular phone calls with the Work Package 
Coordinators (WPC) and Work Package Technical Leaders (WPTL) to identify new risks not yet foreseen and/or 
update those already identified.  
It is particularly important to: 
 
Comunicate any risks that could jeopardize the entire project to the WPCs and the Steering Committee. 
 
Update the risk plan monthly, or as needed,  
 
Implement all possible corrective actions.
