QUALITY MANAGEMENT PLAN 
1.1 
INTRODUCTION 
For the Project Management Institute (PMI), a Quality Management Plan is a document that describes how 
quality will be managed within a project. It outlines the activities, roles, and responsibilities to ensure that the 
final product meets the established quality requirements. 
The use of this Quality Management Plan template has been adopted due to the complexity of the AVANT 
project and since quality activities are of particular importance for the project itself. 
The objectives of this document are:  
 
To outline the quality strategy, approach and process to be used for the project. 
 
To identify the roles and responsibilities related to project quality management. 
 
To identify the major project management artefacts and deliverables. 
 
To define the quality assurance and control activities and to plan them throughout the project. 
 
To support the agreement on project quality requirements and metrics, and the method to evaluate 
them. 
 
To specify the methodology, standards, tools and techniques used to support quality management. 
1.2 
OBJECTIVES 
Scope of this Quality Management Plan for the AVANT project is to provide a reference for the Quality Assurance 
(QA) and Quality Control (QC) processes during the project duration.  
This Milestone (D0.2b) defines the project organisation, procedures, roles, and responsibilities related to the 
quality management that will be carried out and describes how the project quality will be controlled. It is 
intended to ensure that project processes and outputs are monitored and properly reported. It will be used to 
prevent possible deviations from the Work Plan and to grant deliverables of high quality.  
The use of this Quality Management Plan can ensure a better collaboration among all stakeholders.  
The Quality Management Plan applies to project deliverables and project research and FID processes. 
Decisions are allocated to the various management levels of the project to maximise efficiency of the project 
teams. Relevant decisions will be taken based on predetermined conditions, ensuring:  
 
Coordination/cooperation across the project’s WPs and Tasks,  
 
Delivery of expected results at the promised quality and time by using the allocated project resources, 
 
Successful management of any risk or unexpected/unforeseeable situation. 
It involves overseeing all activities needed to maintain a desired level of excellence. This includes creating and 
implementing quality planning and assurance, as well as quality control and quality improvement.  
The main project quality objectives are: 
 
The project's quality characteristics are defined, agreed and achieved throughout the project;

QUALITY MANAGEMENT PROCESS 
The project quality management process comprises all activities (related both to processes and deliverables) that 
will increase the ability to meet the project expected results identified in the Project Management Plan. 
The goals for quality management of information technology projects are to assure:  
 
Project deliverables meet their stated requirements. 
 
Project management processes are appropriately followed. 
Quality management is performed throughout the project lifecycle with special attention to: 
 
Quality Planning – primarily during the project planning process. 
 
Quality Assurance (QA) – primarily during the project execution process. 
 
Quality Control (QC) – primarily during the project monitoring and controlling process. 
 
Verification and Validation  – ideally performed in all project processes except initiation.

Review Process 
1. Each project output will be reviewed by a Reviewer designated in the RASCI matrix. 
2. The Reviewer will evaluate the project output according to the following criteria: 
o 
Content of the project output 
o 
Relevance 
o 
Quality of deliverables 
o

o

o 
Fully Accepted 
o 
Accepted with Reservations 
o 
Rejected 
5. If the project output is accepted with reservations or rejected, the Reviewer will provide specific

7. Once fully accepted, the project output will be subjected to final control by the PMO to ensure a uniform 
layout. 
8. Upon approval, the drafting manager will be responsible for distributing it to all stakeholders. 
2.2.2 
CODE REVIEW 
Guidelines for code reviews are  based on the following guidelines:  
Establish Frequency: 
 
Regularity is Key: Aim for frequent, smaller code reviews rather than infrequent, large ones. This 
allows for quicker feedback and minimizes the risk of accumulating significant technical debt. 
 
Consider the Project: The ideal frequency depends on the project's complexity, team size, and 
development velocity. Daily or every other day reviews might be suitable for fast-paced projects, while 
weekly reviews could be appropriate for others. 
2. Determine Participants: 
 
Involve Relevant Stakeholders: Include developers who wrote the code, reviewers with relevant 
expertise (e.g., senior developers, architects), and potentially other stakeholders like testers or product 
owners. 
 
Pair Programming: If applicable, the pair who worked on the code can participate in the review 
together, explaining their design decisions and answering questions. 
3. Prepare the review: 
 
Code Readability: The developer submitting the code should ensure it is well-formatted, includes clear 
comments, and adheres to coding standards. This makes the review process smoother and more 
efficient. 
 
Unit Tests: Write thorough unit tests for the code under review. This demonstrates that the code 
functions as intended and can help reviewers understand its behavior. 
 
Clear Commit Messages: Use descriptive commit messages that explain the purpose and scope of the 
code changes. 
4. Conducting the Review:

 
Use a Code Review Tool: Utilize a tool like GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket to facilitate the review process. 
These tools provide features like inline comments, diff views, and history tracking. 
 
Focus on Specific Areas: Guide the review by focusing on specific aspects like code style, design, 
performance, security, and testability. 
 
Constructive Feedback: Encourage reviewers to provide specific and actionable feedback. Focus on the 
code itself and avoid personal criticism. 
 
Knowledge Sharing: Use the review as an opportunity for knowledge sharing. Reviewers can explain 
design decisions, share best practices, and teach new techniques. 
5. Managing Identified Defects: 
 
Prioritize Defects: Categorize defects based on severity (e.g., critical, major, minor) and prioritize their 
resolution accordingly. 
 
Assign Ownership: Clearly assign responsibility for fixing each defect to the appropriate developer. 
 
Track and Resolve: Use a bug tracking system (e.g., Jira, Bugzilla) to track and manage defects. Ensure 
that all defects are resolved promptly and thoroughly. 
 
Learn from Defects: Analyze the types of defects that are frequently found during code reviews. Use 
this information to identify areas for improvement in the development process, coding practices, and 
training. 
Example Workflow: 
1. Developer: Submits code for review with clear commit messages and unit tests. 
2. Reviewer: Receives notification, reviews the code, and provides feedback using the code review tool. 
3. Developer: Addresses the feedback, makes necessary changes, and submits the updated code for 
review. 
4. Reviewer: Reviews the updated code and approves or requests further changes. 
5. Developer: Once approved, merges the code into the main branch. 
6. Team: Tracks and resolves identified defects using a bug tracking system.



 
The lead author will refine the deliverable based on the received inputs and will send the updated 
deliverable to the Security Officer (SO) up to 10 days before the deadline; the SO will check the 
deliverable within 5 days.

QUALITY TRAINING AND TECHNICAL MATERIALS 
To ensure project success, the section dedicated to training and technical resources, which will be created, 
provides detailed guidelines for developing and utilizing project-specific documentation. This chapter will be 
further structured in subsequent releases ensuring adherence to all KPIs indicated in the project portfolio. 
Standards for the training materials that will be produced will be outlined, in accordance with the following 
specifications: 
 
Evaluation of documents based on criteria such as clarity, conciseness, and comprehensibility. 
 
Design of engaging and interactive training sessions. 
 
Offering training in various formats, including in-person sessions, online courses, and webinars. 
 
Customization of training content to meet the specific needs of the target audience, whether sector-
specific or cross-sector. 
 
Regular updating of training to incorporate the latest developments in the AVANT project. 
 
Availability of training resources in both English and Italian, taking into account the target audience. 
 
Collaboration with universities, research institutes, and industry associations to expand the training 
offerings and promote the dissemination of information about the AVANT project. 
Additionally, to ensure the maintenance of high standards in the quality of training resources, quality 
improvement cycles will be implemented. These cycles will involve a series of structured phases to continuously 
monitor and optimize both the resources and the training activities. Key monitoring parameters will include 
content, delivery, assessment, and feedback. 
Through the implementation of these quality improvement cycles, it will be possible to ensure that the training 
resources not only meet the initial standards but also adapt continuously to effectively address user needs and 
the evolving dynamics of the AVANT project. 
 
3.1 
EVALUATION PARAMETERS  
3.1.1 
CONTENT 
 
Accuracy: Training content must be rigorously verified to ensure the accuracy of information and 
alignment with AVANT project objectives. 
 
Up-to-dateness: Training materials must be periodically updated to reflect the latest project 
developments and technological advancements. 
 
Relevance: Content must be closely related to the training needs of the developers and engineers 
involved in the project. 
 
Organization and comprehensibility: Materials must be structured clearly and concisely, facilitating 
understanding even for users with diverse technical backgrounds. 
3.1.2 
DELIVERY 
 
Engagement: Training should include interactive and practical activities, such as using the digital 
playground, to maximize learning and participant engagement.

4 
MEETING EFFECTIVENESS RULES 
4.1 
MEETING REPRESENTATIVES 
Each person involved in the project, representing one or more workgroups, must designate at least one 
representative to attend the meetings in case they cannot attend. 
4.2 
MEETING NOTICE 
The PMO group, or whoever calls a meeting, sends a written notice to each member of the relevant Consortium 
Body. Project Board: 14 days for ordinary meetings, 7 days for extraordinary meetings. TMC: 14 days for ordinary 
meetings, 7 days for extraordinary meetings. 
4.3 
AGENDA 
The PMO group, or whoever calls a meeting, prepares and sends an agenda. 
Project Board: 7 days before the meeting. 
TMC: 7 days before the meeting. Agendas for both bodies must clearly indicate any item requiring a decision. 
4.4 
ADDING AGENDA ITEMS 
Any member may add an item to the agenda by written notice to all other members. 
Project Board: 5 days before the meeting. 
TMC: 5 days before the meeting. Alternatively, during the meeting, a new item can be added by unanimous 
agreement of members present or represented. 
4.5 
MEETING FORMAT 
Meetings are primarily conducted via teleconference, videoconference, or other telecommunication means. 
4.6 
BINDING DECISIONS 
Decisions made during these meetings become binding only when the corresponding minutes are accepted or 
whenever alternatively decided by the meeting participants due to urgency criteria.

5 
Integration of the Communication and Dissemination Plan, Collaboration and 
Community Engagement Plan  
The AVANT project's Quality Plan is complemented by the Communication,and Dissemination Plan and the 
Collaboration and Community Engagement Plan. These documents, attached to the Quality Plan, define the 
strategies and actions for effective project communication, collaboration between partners, and dissemination of 
the results achieved. 
In particular, the Communication and Dissemination Plan outlines the methods of internal and external 
communication for the project, defining the communication channels, roles, and responsibilities of the partners 
involved in disseminating the project results.  Moreover, it specifies the activities and tools used to maximize the 
project's visibility and its impact on the scientific community and the general public. The collaboration and 
Community Engagement Plan summarizes the various ways the AVANT team plans to build the foundation for, 
and support, effective internal and external collaboration and engagement across the lifespan of the IPCEI 
initiative.The integration of these plans into the Quality Plan ensures a comprehensive and coordinated approach 
to project management, promoting transparency, collaboration, and the valorization of the results achieved.
