INTRODUCTION 
1.1 
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
The Collaboration and Community Engagement Plan, annexed to the Quality Management Plan, described in this 
report covers the liaison and co-operation activities of the AVANT project. 
The Collaboration and Community Engagement Plan summarizes the various ways the AVANT team plans to 
build the foundation for, and support, effective internal and external collaboration and engagement across the 
lifespan of the IPCEI initiative. 
1.2 
INTENDED AUDIENCE 
This document is intended for the units involved in the AVANT project development in agreement with the 
project portfolio accepted by the EC and for the open-source community, other companies and organizations 
interested in using AVANT solutions. 
1.3 
STRUCTURE OF THE DOCUMENT 
This document is divided into 2 main sections: 
Collaboration: this section describes the collaborative approach adopted in the project, defines the stakeholder 
groups and the way collaboration takes play at various levels and with different stakeholders. In particular: 
• 
Internal Collaboration among R&D laboratories, Business line, and Subsidiaries,  
• 
Collaboration with External IPCEI Partners (Direct and Indirect) 
• 
Collaboration with open-Source Communities 
• 
Collaboration with academic institutions  
• 
Collaboration with Public Institutions and agencies 
Community Engagement: This section describes how it intends to engage the community, promoting 
inclusivity, encourage collaboration to actively participate in the processes.

COLLABORATION: GUIDING PRINCIPLES AND APPROACH 
2.1 
MAIN AREAS OF COLLABORATION 
This section briefly recalls AVANT main collaboration areas. 
AVANT will deliver an integrated SW solution to build and deploy Digital Twins (DTs from now on) articulated in 
8 specific SW platforms, to be realized in 8 WPs, all with RDI and FDI phases. 
The innovations proposed are a) creating a flexible interoperability layer, foundation for advanced computing 
(WP1); b) leveraging WP1 to create a data continuum considering data produced at edge/far-edge (WP2); c) 
secure DTs in collaborative cyber-physical environments and apply DTs for security (WP3); d) enable modular, 
low-cost, flexible and scalable DTs in manufacturing, (WP4); e) use DTs and AI for cultural heritage (CH) 
conservation, preservation, restoration, and fruition (WP5); f) UDT for an effective multi-level data-driven 
governance, for territorial long-term resilience and sustainability strategies (WP6); g) exploit DTs to enable 
improved operations in decentralized energy systems(WP7); h) provide a comprehensive platform for evidence-
based medicine (WP8). 
The 8 main areas of collaboration coincide with the 8 AVANT objectives: 
• 
A-Obj.1 - Resource discovery, observability, orchestration, and benchmarking techniques across the 
computing continuum from IoT deployments to the cloud (WP1). 
• 
A-Obj.2 - Solving data and computing interoperability, integration, and heterogeneity issues to create a 
cloud-native ecosystem platform for data value creation (WP2) 
• 
A-Obj.3 - Cybersecurity of DTs and DTs for cybersecurity (WP3) 
• 
A-Obj.4 - A Modular approach for Industrial DTs (WP4) 
• 
A-Obj.5 - DTs and AI for Cultural Heritage (WP5) 
• 
A-Obj.6 - Urban DTs for simulation, monitoring, and data analysis (WP6) 
• 
A-Obj.7 - Community-aware DTs for optimized management of decentralized energy systems (WP7) 
• 
A-Obj.8 – Enhanced EHRs supporting personalized medicine through DTs and AI (WP8). 
2.2 
APPROACHES AND AVENUES FOR CULTIVATING COLLABORATIVE PARTNERSHIP 
Avant strives to ensure the full implementation of its workplan through efficient and effective collaborations and 
partnerships. In doing so, AVANT will take a step-wise approach in pursuing collaborative partnerships, whether 
on the activity, thematic, or programmatic basis, to promote learning, innovation, and continuous improvements 
over time, and ensure timely and fit-for-purpose adjustments to its approach in the face of emerging priorities 
and mandates. 
The approach includes the following phases: 
Identification Phase 
Develop a comprehensive partner matrix that aligns with AVANT's objectives for cooperative partnerships. This 
strategic mapping ensures that every potential partner is evaluated against AVANT's goals, fostering a network 
of collaborations that are both relevant and effective.

Engagement & Scoping Phase 
Proactively initiate contact with prospective partners to establish collaborative relationships. These partnerships 
should resonate with the core values and principles of AVANT. By doing so, AVANT can ensure that the 
collaborations are not only strategic but also adhere to its foundational ethos. 
Definition & Refinement Phase 
Agree upon common goals, deliverables, schedules, roles, responsibilities, and benchmarks for progress. These 
will be outlined through appropriate channels and methods of collaboration. This step is crucial for setting clear 
expectations and creating a framework for accountability and success. 
Promotion Phase 
Actively promote collaborative projects using various platforms and methodically share the results. This 
promotion will be integrated with AVANT's communication and outreach strategies, thereby maximizing visibility 
and impact. 
Monitoring & Learning Phase 
Continuously track progress and solicit input from both partners and key stakeholders to guarantee the effective 
execution of activities. This process will be in sync AVANT's KPI allowing for real-time adjustments and 
improvements. 
Impact Phase 
Explore and establish mechanisms to sustain the collaborative efforts or integrate them into other ENG initiatives 
or those of pertinent technology entities. This approach not only extends the lifespan of successful collaborations 
but also ensures that they contribute to broader technological advancements. 
This strategy underscores the importance of a structured yet dynamic approach to collaboration, where each 
phase builds upon the previous one, leading to a cohesive and impactful partnership ecosystem. This emphasizes 
the need for alignment with the AVANT Communication & Dissemination Plan, ultimately aiming to create 
lasting technological advancements. The argument for such a strategy lies in its potential to streamline efforts, 
leverage diverse expertise, and amplify the outcomes of collaborative endeavors.

Internal Collaboration  
Internal Collaboration involves the coordinated efforts between different departments within the Engineering 
Group, such as R&D laboratories, business units, and controlled companies. This type of collaboration is crucial 
for several reasons: 
R&D laboratories work closely with business units to ensure that new products and technologies are aligned with 
market needs and business strategies. This synergy accelerates innovation and helps in developing cutting-edge 
solutions. 
Moreover, internal collaboration fosters a culture of knowledge sharing. Business units can provide valuable 
market insights to R&D teams, while R&D can share technical advancements and research findings that can be 
applied across the organization. 
Finally, internal collaboration helps in aligning the strategies of different units, ensuring that everyone is on the 
same page and working towards the same objectives. 
Collaboration with external IPCEI partners  
IPCEI stands for "Important Projects of Common European Interest." These projects represent a significant 
contribution to economic growth, jobs, the green and digital transition, and competitiveness for the Union 
industry and economy. IPCEIs facilitate the collaboration of knowledge, expertise, financial resources, and 
economic actors throughout the Union, creating positive spillover effects for the entire Union1   
IPCEI projects must encourage cross-border cooperation, bringing together a diverse range of stakeholders, 
including governments, private companies, and research institutions.  
IPCEIs are a testament to the EU's commitment to collaborative innovation and shared economic prosperity. They 
are designed to tackle large-scale projects that no single Member State could achieve on its own, thus 
strengthening the EU's position in the global economy. 
In AVANT, two types of collaborations with IPCEI partner companies will be considered: 
• 
Direct Partnerships: These involve formal agreements with partners in the Important Projects of 
Common European Interest (IPCEI) to work on specific aspects of the project, sharing knowledge, 
technology, and resources.  
• 
Indirect Partnerships: These might include less formal collaborations with entities that are part of the 
broader IPCEI network, potentially offering complementary expertise or resources. 
Collaboration with European Open-Source Movement 
AVANT is designed to generate spillover effects and raise awareness about the European open-source 
movement, which, according to the latest EC reports, acts as a significant multiplier and creator of spillover.

Collaboration with Academic Institutions 
Collaboration with universities and research centers is essential for fostering innovation and advancing 
knowledge. By working closely with academic institutions, Engineering can gain access to specialized expertise 
and state-of-the-art facilities that may not be available in-house.  
This collaboration often leads to the development of new products, processes, and technologies. Universities 
provide access to cutting-edge research and academic expertise, which can be invaluable for both the theoretical 
and applied aspects of a project.  
Moreover, collaborations can serve as a conduit for recruiting new talent from among students and researchers.   
Collaboration with MIMIT and European Commission 
A governance framework will be established to oversee the implementation and monitoring of IPCEI CIS.  
The Member States are committed to this framework and will support it by aligning their national strategies and 
fostering the development of innovative technologies throughout the cloud edge value chain. 
Collaboration with target group  
Target Groups represent potential users and customers in terms of culture, experience, language, geography and 
in some cases also the domain of activity.  
• 
IT providers are typically large organizations willing to (re)use AVANT solutions (especially from WP1-3) 
to increase their offering towards their end-customers,  
• 
SMEs/startups include both IT providers and final users, which, due to their reduced dimensions or 
limited resources, would use AVANT results to radically improve their business.  
• 
End Users are public or private organizations which are focused on one of the application domains 
included in AVANT, subject of WP4-8. 
• 
Public administrations: Working with public institutions ensures that the research project aligns with 
governmental policies and priorities, which can be essential for funding and support. Public 
administrations often serve as a bridge between researchers and the community, facilitating the 
integration of research outcomes with public services and infrastructure.

7.1.1 
GOVERNANCE OF THE IPCEI-CIS 
For the management, implementation and monitoring of the IPCEI CIS, the EC has established a governance 
structure organized around main bodies: 
IPCEI General Assembly (GA)  
The GA is a forum for common understanding and exchange of all IPCEI CIS partners, Member States and the 
Commission. Within the General Assembly (GA), all participating members vote to appoint the Chair and Vice 
Chair of the Facilitation Group as their representatives. The Supervisory Board's strategy for the IPCEI CIS will be 
unveiled, along with a comprehensive update on the IPCEI CIS's advancements. Additionally, the GA is 
responsible for selecting the host for the subsequent GA meeting. 
The General Assembly meets at least once a year during the General Assembly Annual Meeting. The Supervisory 
Board can summon autonomously or upon request by the FG additional General Assembly meetings. 
The IPCEI CIS General Assembly (GA) includes:  
• 
All partners involved in the IPCEI (both direct participants and indirect partners),  
• 
representatives from all Member States engaged in the IPCEI CIS ecosystem (whether as direct 
participants or indirect partners), who act as observers without voting rights,  
• 
and the Commission, which also acts as an observer without voting rights. 
IPCEI Supervisory Board (SB) 
The SB's role is to oversee, monitor, and ensure the overall implementation of IPCEI CIS. This includes tracking 
the progress of individual projects by participating entities and the IPCEI CIS as a whole. The SB will be 
responsible for annual reporting to the Commission, based on information from the FG and CO. Additionally, the 
SB will offer strategic guidance on the master project plan, technological decisions, and standards alignment. It 
will also handle disputes among IPCEI participants. The SB's decisions will be binding for all IPCEI CIS participants. 
Generally, the SB will meet twice a year, but it can also convene extraordinary sessions to address issues like 
delays in the integrated project's implementation. 
It is composed of: 
o 
The Member States involved in the integrated project (whether as direct participants or indirect 
partners), each having one vote. 
o 
The IPCEI CIS Facilitation Group (FG), made up of members elected from the GA to represent the 
GA’s partner members in the SB. The FG is represented at SB meetings by its Chair and Vice 
Chair and holds two votes.

8.1 
COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT APPROACH 
This paragraph serves as a resource to help to think through the process of developing a AVANT community engagement 
plan by providing questions and key points for consideration. 
An engagement plan outlines a set of specific outreach and participation strategies with assigned roles and a timeline. 
The AVANT community engagement approach is composed of 4 steps. 
STEP 1: UNDERSTAND WHAT YOU KNOW AND DON’T KNOW ABOUT THE PROJECT 
Before developing outreach strategies, consider: 
• 
What is the project’s goal for the community? 
• 
What are the anticipated impacts? 
• 
What is the current level of community awareness about the project? 
• 
How effective were past community engagement efforts for similar projects? 
• 
What are the possible consequences of not engaging the community? 
• 
How can the project timeline accommodate community engagement? 
• 
What information is still unknown about the project? 
STEP 2: IDENTIFY WHO TO ENGAGE 
Develop an understanding of either or both of the following: 
• 
The relationship between the level of project impact on community stakeholders and their current level of 
engagement (illustration 1). 
• 
The relationship between stakeholders’ influence on project outcomes and their current level of engagement 
(illustration 2). 
Consider: 
• 
Which community members/groups will be most impacted? 
• 
Which groups are already engaged or disengaged? 
• 
Which groups have a high influence on project outcomes? 
• 
Which groups need to be empowered? 
• 
Which groups are easy or difficult to reach? 
• 
Which groups do you already have relationships with, and which need new connections?
